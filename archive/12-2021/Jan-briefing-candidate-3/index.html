One of the reasons that I have emphasized that it is a very good idea
to have a bootable flash drive with your current operating system on
it (at least a half dozen times) is you can use it to boot to a live
system and then install the boot repair software on your hard drive. 
From there you can repair your system without having to reinstall the
entire system thereby saving you the time and effort required to do
so.  Excellent idea George we will discuss the PPAs at our January

Rex advice is very wise.
In the time of the BIOS you could back up the MBR (466 bytes) or the
MBR + partition (512 bytes) table and often the problem was resolved
but.. those times are over and now most of the systems come with UEFI
the back is complex. Of course on the top you may have corruption of
grub2, SO.. having a boot flash drive with your OS makes life far saver
and simple. Under OpenSUSE I can do it in two way, most likely is the
same in Ubuntu;
1. I can have a live OpenSUSE where the whole sytem is available
2. I can use the bootable OpenSUSE installation and from there I can
repair the problem.
In general #1 is the simple and the least likely you can scr.......
The most common situation is when you have a good running linux and you
decided to install win10, win manager will take over you will not have
access to linux. Yes you can conf the win manager but.... In this case
I load the OpenSuSE live, go to grub2, and it will find win10 and next
time you do the IPL you will have grub menu wiht linux and windows.

So do not delay, consider this advice as urgent.
