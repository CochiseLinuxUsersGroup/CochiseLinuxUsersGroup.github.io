Inaugural Meeting 20 Aug 2015

From MajB · Aug 21, 2015

The inaugural meeting of the Cochise Linux User Group (CLUG) was held on 20 August 2005 from 4 - 5:30 p.m. at the University of Arizona South, room C165A – North. It was attended by 8 people. Among the topics discussed were frequency, length, and location of future meetings. It was decided that for the time being future meetings will be held monthly on the second Wednesday of each month starting at 5 p.m. at the University of Arizona South, room C165A – North. The location may vary depending upon the schedule of classes at the University. A presentation should be included for each meeting and should last 15-30 minutes. We will look for external presenters for topics where members do not have any expertise. The first presentation will be given by Rex Bouwense on Introduction to Linux. It was also decided that there is no desire or need for any fees for membership or refreshments for future meetings. A discussion on a need for any Group leader(s) was put on hold for some future meeting.
The next meeting will be on 9 September.

Re: Inaugural Meeting 20 Aug 2015

From Meditz, Joseph · Aug 22, 2015

Hi Major B,

I've posted an item on the EGR 122 Programming for Engineering and Science student's Cochise College Moodle site.  

There are a number of Running Start students in the class, one of which, Sarah, is a Cyber Patriot.  When I told the class we would be learning Linux and using the command line to compile our code, Sarah said she loves the command line!  

I told them that I am attending as a learner and that you would be giving an Introduction to Linux presentation.  I don't know how many of the students interested in Linux will attend.  After all, those Running Start students are taking a full HS course load as well as this course.  So I told them that if they are interested in Linux and can't attend to join the mailing list and keep up with what's going on.

I told them that we are fortunate to have the CLUG and that while California and New York have Linux interest groups you often have to travel miles to attend them.

V/r,
Joe
